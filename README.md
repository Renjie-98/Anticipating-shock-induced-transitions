# Anticipating shock-induced transitions in complex interconnected systems from limited information

The coding for anticipating shock-induced transitions

Renjie Xu, Davide Zanchetta, Mauro Faccin, Jichao Li, Kewei Yang, Manlio De Domenico*


### Abstract: 

From molecular dysfunction in cells to species loss in food webs and failures of engineered networks, complex systems face perturbations that push them into states where functionality might be impaired. The ability to anticipate those states is essential to plan interventions for mitigation and recovery. However, existing approaches are primarily designed to address specific shock types or rely on system-specific dynamical assumptions, limiting their ability to learn system-wide perturbation responses from limited observations within a unified prediction framework. To overcome these limitations, we use a minimal framework to capture the causal mapping between initial and perturbed states from data, without requiring prior knowledge of the underlying dynamics, thereby enabling efficient and interpretable anticipation of network responses to diverse perturbations. Theoretical analysis and experiments across complex dynamics and real-world networks show that our framework captures how perturbations propagate through the system in response to a variety of shocks, enabling accurate, system-wide anticipation of perturbed states.
